Global Tech News — 4 September 2026

By Marz Technology | 4 September 2026

1. NVIDIA Reports Record USD 48B Revenue on AI Chip Demand

NVIDIA has posted record quarterly revenue of USD 48 billion, driven by unprecedented demand for AI training chips from cloud providers and enterprises worldwide. The company announced next-generation Blackwell Ultra chips shipping in Q4 2026.

Our POV: AI chip demand shows no signs of slowing. For businesses, this means AI infrastructure costs may remain high in the short term, but the performance gains are making AI more practical for real-world applications.

Implications for Business:

  • Opportunity: New chip generation = more powerful AI at lower power consumption
  • Risk: AI infrastructure costs remain a barrier for smaller businesses
  • Action: Consider cloud-based AI services instead of building your own infrastructure

2. EU AI Act Enforcement Begins — Global Compliance Implications

The European Union's AI Act has entered full enforcement, requiring companies using AI in the EU market to comply with transparency, risk assessment, and data governance requirements. Companies worldwide are scrambling to ensure compliance.

Our POV: The EU AI Act will likely influence AI regulations across Asia, including Malaysia. Companies building or deploying AI should proactively adopt responsible AI practices now.

Implications for Business:

  • Opportunity: First-movers in responsible AI gain trust and competitive advantage
  • Risk: Non-compliance could result in fines up to 7% of global revenue
  • Action: Audit your AI usage and ensure transparency in AI-driven decisions

3. Microsoft Azure Expands Southeast Asia Cloud Region

Microsoft has announced a new Azure cloud region in Southeast Asia, offering lower latency and data residency options for businesses in Malaysia, Indonesia, and the Philippines. The region includes dedicated AI compute capacity.

Our POV: Major cloud providers investing in SEA infrastructure is great news for Malaysian businesses. Lower latency, data sovereignty, and competitive pricing are all within reach.

Implications for Business:

  • Opportunity: Better cloud performance and data residency compliance for Malaysian businesses
  • Risk: Multi-cloud complexity increases as more providers enter the market
  • Action: Evaluate cloud migration or optimisation with the new regional options

Key Takeaways

  1. AI infrastructure investment continues to break records globally
  2. AI regulation is real and coming to Southeast Asia — prepare now
  3. Cloud expansion in SEA means better infrastructure for Malaysian businesses
